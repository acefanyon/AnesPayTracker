Design Review · AnesPayTracker

A calmer, faster AnesPay

A read of the current SwiftUI codebase, the friction in its flows, and four iPhone mockups of a redesign built around the three questions the app exists to answer: what did I earn, when do I get paid, and how close is my next bonus?

Reviewed Aug 8, 2026 · branch claude/app-ui-ux-review-pt0vu6 · 15 Swift files, iOS 17+, SwiftData + CloudKit

01 · What this app is

A shift-pay ledger for a contract anesthesiologist

AnesPay tracks worked days for a 1099 anesthesiologist across employers (staffing groups with a pay cadence and contacts) and sites (hospitals paying per-day or per-hour). Each shift records a day fraction or hours, optional "splash" bonuses (high-need day incentives), a source note documenting who offered the shift and how, and a full edit audit trail — it's as much a dispute-evidence ledger as a pay calculator. A streak engine awards flat bonuses for working N days in a window (e.g. 4 days in 14 → $500) and attributes them to the triggering shift. Views: a month calendar, pay-period groupings, streak progress, a filterable report with PDF export, and a settings area. Everything is local SwiftData with optional CloudKit sync — no accounts, no SDKs. The engineering is genuinely solid; the friction is almost all in information architecture and flow.

02 · Interface refinements

Where the current UI fights the user

Individually small, together these are why the app feels unfinished rather than broken.

Navigation & structure

  • No home screen. The app opens on the calendar; earnings, payday, and streak status live on three separate tabs. The most common question requires the most navigation.
  • Five tabs is two too many. "Report" is a task (export), not a place; Settings doesn't earn a tab slot. Merge Pay Periods + Report into one Earnings tab.
  • The floating "+" covers content. It hovers 72pt above the tab bar, overlapping list rows and the month summary card, and duplicates nothing visible — if it's the hot path, put it in the tab bar.

The entry flow (hot path)

  • Blank until you pick a site. The form should preselect the last-used site so a repeat shift is three taps: open, confirm, save.
  • Save is a tiny toolbar word. The commit action for the app's core flow deserves a full-width thumb-reach button pinned above the keyboard, with the live total beside it.
  • Streak feedback arrives too late. A blocking alert fires after saving. Show "this shift completes your 4-in-14 streak (+$500)" before the save, in the total footer.
  • Dates are a full picker. Most entries are today or yesterday — offer those as one-tap chips.

Visual language

  • Mixed iconography. "🎯" emoji, SF Symbols, and orange/green/teal are used interchangeably. Pick one semantic map: teal = money, amber = streak, green = paid.
  • Cents everywhere. $1,800.00 six times per screen is noise; whole dollars until the PDF.
  • Cards at 6% opacity barely separate from the background, and several labels use .tertiary — low-contrast for a glance-heavy app used in hospital corridors.
  • Unlabeled toggles (Toggle("", …)) and 9pt calendar chips fail the VoiceOver / tap-target bar the README aspires to.

Calendar & review

  • Day cells aren't tappable — only the tiny chips inside them are. Tapping a day should open a day sheet: its shifts, or "log a shift for this day."
  • Pay periods open collapsed, hiding all detail behind a tap; the current period should arrive expanded with its expected payday visible.
  • No payday anywhere. The app knows the cadence but never says when money actually lands — the single most useful date it could show.

A new Home screen answers the three core questions without navigation. All mockups use the app's real seed data and iOS-native visual grammar.

04 · Missing features

What the app should learn to do

Ordered by how directly each serves the core job of getting a contractor paid correctly.

Money truth

  • Payment reconciliation — mark periods paid, flag overdue ones, record received-vs-expected discrepancies. The audit trail exists; the money loop doesn't close.
  • Payday dates — the app computes period bounds but never shows when payment lands.
  • Rate history with effective dates — sites change rates; today a rate edit silently rewrites the past (see problem #1 below).
  • CSV export + full data backup — PDF is for humans; the accountant wants CSV, and irreplaceable dispute evidence deserves an export path.

Speed & habit

  • Duplicate last shift — "same as Tuesday" is the most common entry.
  • Streak deadline notifications — "1 shift by Thursday earns $500" is the app's most valuable possible push.
  • Lock-screen widget / Apple Watch entry — log a shift walking out of the OR.
  • Search and undo — no way to find a shift by note text, and delete is permanent with no undo window.

The 1099 reality

  • Quarterly tax view — YTD income by quarter with a set-aside percentage is table stakes for contractor finance.
  • Mileage / expense capture — she drives between sites; deductions live where the shifts already are.
  • Face ID app lock — full income records on an unlocked phone.
  • Multi-employer summary — per-employer YTD once a second employer exists.

05 · Foreseeable problems

Issues visible in the code today

Found while reading, not running — each names the file so it can be verified in Xcode.

High

Editing an old shift silently repriced it

On save, AddShiftView.swift:181 sets shift.baseAmount = site.baseAmount — the site's current rate. Fix a typo in a note on a January shift after a March raise and its historical pay changes with no trace, in an app whose purpose is dispute evidence. Shifts must keep their snapshotted rate unless the user explicitly changes it; long-term, sites need dated rate history.

High

Biweekly periods are anchored to January 1, not a real payday

StreakEngine.payPeriodBounds counts 14-day blocks from Jan 1 of the shift's year. Real biweekly schedules anchor to an employer's actual payday, so every computed period is likely wrong — and because the anchor resets each year, a period spanning New Year's splits into two, breaking both pay-period totals and pay-period streak windows. The employer model needs an anchor date.

High

Seed data blocks real onboarding

SeedData inserts a fake employer on first launch, and ContentView shows the setup wizard only when no employers exist — so a real user never sees onboarding and starts with fictional data (which CloudKit will then sync). Gate seeding behind #if DEBUG or an explicit "Explore with sample data" choice on the welcome screen.

Medium

Streaks count shifts, not days — and ignore fractions

StreakEngine.shiftsInWindow counts shift records: two half-days logged on the same date count as two "days" toward a 4-day streak, and a ¼ day counts the same as a full day. Whether that matches the employer's actual bonus terms should be a per-rule setting (distinct days; minimum fraction to qualify).

Medium

Stacked streak bonuses misattribute their rule

When two rules trigger on the same shift, bonus amounts accumulate but streakRuleTriggeredID keeps only the last rule (StreakEngine.swift:45), so the Streaks history under-reports one rule and the shift detail can show a total that no single rule explains.

Medium

The audit trail misses the edits that matter

buildEditSummary records only date/fraction/hour changes; changing a splash from $350 to $700 logs "Minor edit." For a dispute ledger, every money-affecting field change should be captured with before → after values.

Medium

Every save is try?

All persistence uses try? modelContext.save() — a failed save (CloudKit quota, migration hiccup, disk pressure) loses a shift silently. The hot path should surface failures and retry; the data is too valuable to drop quietly.

Low

Timestamps will drift across time zones

Shifts store full Date timestamps and every grouping compares against local-calendar day bounds. A shift logged late at night, or while traveling across time zones, can land in the wrong day, month, or pay period. Storing a calendar day (y/m/d) rather than an instant is the durable fix.

Low

Destructive cascade with no export ramp

Deleting an employer cascades away all its sites, shifts, and history behind a single confirmation. With no backup/export feature, one confident tap can erase years of dispute evidence. Offer an export-first prompt, or soft-delete with a grace period.
